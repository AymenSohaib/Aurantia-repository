# Satin Current

*An algorithmic philosophy for Aurantia*

Satin Current treats light on cloth as a computation. A bolt of fabric is nothing but a field of heights, and what the eye reads as luxury is the way that field bends light: dull where the surface lies flat, blazing where it folds toward you. The movement renders that fold with hundreds of parallel threads laid across a dark ground. No thread knows it is part of a drape. Each simply follows a height function sampled from layered harmonics and low-frequency noise, and the cloth appears only in aggregate, in the places where neighbouring threads crowd together and turn their faces to the light.

The field is built from two kinds of motion held in tension. A slow sinusoid gives the drape its long, breathing gesture, a single wave that crosses the whole frame. Beneath it, octaves of smooth noise add the irregular gathers that real satin makes when it slips over a hand. A twist parameter offsets each thread's phase from its neighbour's, so the ribbon rolls over itself and the sheen travels diagonally instead of sitting in stripes. Every value is chosen against the others: amplitude, fold frequency, twist and density form a balance that took patient, meticulous tuning, the kind of painstaking calibration a master of computational aesthetics spends far longer on than the code that executes it.

Light is calculated, never painted. For every segment the algorithm derives the surface normal from the thread's slope, measures it against a fixed light from the upper left, and raises the result to a sheen exponent. Low exponents give velvet: soft, diffuse, forgiving. High exponents give satin: narrow ridges of near-white gold racing along each crest. The palette is restricted to a ground, a body gold and a highlight, and all colour between them is interpolated by that lighting term, so tone always means geometry. Beneath the threads a broad, blurred aura of the same gold rises from the densest part of the band, the glow that gives the movement its warmth.

At the brightest crests the system leaves evidence of what the cloth carries. Where the lighting term peaks above a threshold, a few four-pointed glints ignite, as sparks off cut zircon. Rarer still, a pearl settles onto a thread: a small sphere shaded with the same light direction, so it belongs to the scene instead of floating on it. These accents are scarce by design. Their scarcity is the point, and it is governed by thresholds, never by decoration.

Everything is seeded. A seed fixes the noise field, the phase offsets, the placement of every glint and pearl, so one number reproduces one bolt of cloth exactly, and the next number weaves another. The work should feel inevitable at every seed: a meticulously crafted algorithm, refined through countless iterations, whose output looks as if a master spent days choosing where the light should fall, when in truth the light fell exactly where the mathematics said it must.
